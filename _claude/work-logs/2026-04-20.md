## Tier II Planning — Interval Recognition

### Core Concept
Two phases per question:
1. **Melodic first** — hear the two notes played one after the other (sequential, like Tier I)
2. **Then harmonic** — hear them played at the same time
3. **Identify the interval** — what's the distance between the notes?

### Difficulty Waves

**Wave 1 — The "perfect" intervals (easier to hear):**
- Perfect unison (same note)
- Perfect octave
- Perfect fifth
- Perfect fourth

**Wave 2 — Major intervals:**
- Major 2nd
- Major 3rd
- Major 6th
- Major 7th

**Wave 3 — Minor intervals:**
- Minor 2nd
- Minor 3rd
- Minor 6th
- Minor 7th

**Wave 4 — The hard ones:**
- Augmented 4th / Diminished 5th (tritone — same sound, two names)
- Diminished and augmented variants

### User Context
User did interval identification back in 7th grade orchestra but the conductor made it boring and never explained things well. Admits he didn't listen either. Wants a working understanding, not mastery — enough to recognize common intervals and understand the vocabulary. This is about reclaiming something that was poorly taught, not becoming a theory expert.

### Design Notes for Tier II
- Keep the same calm/clinical aesthetic
- Same 5-minute session philosophy
- Drill + Quiz modes like Tier I
- Harmonic mode makes its comeback here — it's relevant for intervals (hear them ring together)
- Consider progressive unlocking: start with Wave 1, unlock Wave 2 after some accuracy threshold
- The humming feature could carry over — hum the second note of the interval
